How we work

Three ways we're different from the big-box brokerages

We only take listings we've walked

No agent at Wren & Oak lists a house they haven't personally toured, including the basement and the roofline. If we wouldn't put an offer in ourselves, we'll tell you why.

One agent, start to close

The person who shows you the house is the person who negotiates the offer and sits with you at closing — not a rotating cast handed off between departments.

We read the inspection report first

Before we let a client sign anything, an agent goes through the inspection line by line with them, in plain language, and flags what's actually worth negotiating over.

No pressure toward a bigger loan

We're not paid more for a higher sale price on your end as a buyer. If a smaller, plainer house fits your budget better, we'll say so.

Where we work

Five neighborhoods, and nowhere else

Alder Park — Craftsman & foursquare Southbridge — brick rowhouses Union Hill — postwar bungalows Cobble Quarter — converted warehouse lofts Northgate — mid-century ranch

We turned down expanding into Millhaven's newer subdivisions twice, because keeping the map small is the only way three agents can know every block by name — which sidewalks flood in April, which HOA actually enforces its rules, which streets are about to get a lot noisier once the corner lot sells.

If you're moving into one of these five neighborhoods, chances are one of us has already sat on a client's porch there. If you're moving out of Millhaven entirely, we'll say so plainly and point you to an agent we trust in your next city, instead of stretching to cover ground we don't actually know.
